## 1. The Phoenix Protocol

The Phoenix Protocol is the ultimate fail-safe and self-correction mechanism within the TAS architecture. It ensures that if the system's structural integrity is compromised beyond the threshold of normal metabolic recovery, a deterministic rollback occurs to preserve the core invariants.

### The P2 Hard Rollback and the 4-tiered SLA
The protocol defines a rigorous Service Level Agreement (SLA) with four tiers of escalating response, culminating in the P2 Hard Rollback, which physically severs compromised nodes and restores the last known valid state.

* **Testable Invariant:** Any system state classified as structurally compromised (Tier 4 failure) must trigger a deterministic P2 Hard Rollback to a provably valid prior state.
* **Inputs:** System health metrics, cryptographic proof validation streams, error logs.
* **Transformations:** Continuous evaluation against the 4-tiered SLA threshold bounds.
* **Failure Condition:** If an invalid state is detected and not resolved within Tier 1-3 SLA timeframes, or if a Tier 4 fatal error occurs, the system forcefully executes the P2 rollback. Failure to rollback constitutes a total system collapse.

## 2. The Triadic Knowledge Engine

The Triadic Knowledge Engine is the primary metabolic processor of the architecture. It ingests unverified data, processes it through the cryptographic constraints of Phase 2, and outputs structurally sound, verified state updates.

### The DecisionStrainGauge
The engine utilizes the DecisionStrainGauge to measure the structural stress placed on the system by any given operation. It ensures that no single process or node can exceed the physical constraints defined by the Nordland Hamiltonian.

* **Testable Invariant:** Every data transformation process must pass through the Triadic Knowledge Engine and register a strain value below the critical threshold on the DecisionStrainGauge.
* **Inputs:** Raw data payload, environmental context vectors.
* **Transformations:** Triadic processing (ingestion, structural validation, cryptographic binding) and continuous calculation of the resulting strain vector.
* **Failure Condition:** If the DecisionStrainGauge registers a value exceeding the predefined safety limits, the transformation is aborted and logged as an over-strain event.

## Beyond Scaling World Models

The task is no longer to scale larger world models of the same problem. World models can simulate, predict, and plan, but simulation alone cannot establish admissibility.

A world model may identify what could happen. It cannot, by itself, prove what should be allowed to become canonical action.

Phase 3 therefore shifts the center of gravity from predictive simulation to trajectory admissibility. The system does not ask whether a proposed action is plausible, fluent, or strategically useful. It asks whether the transition is authorized, constraint-bound, lineage-preserving, and proof-bearing.

The DecisionStrainGauge exists at this boundary. It measures whether a proposed transition deforms the verified trajectory before the ZK circuit is asked to prove it. If the strain is low, the transition may attempt proof. If the strain is high, it requires witness or escalation. If the strain is fatal, Phoenix routes it directly into the Refusal Ledger.

This prevents world-model imagination from becoming execution without pedigree.

```text
Prediction is not permission.
Simulation is not lineage.
Plausibility is not admissibility.
No proof → no fold → no execution.
```

**Operational Definition:**
```text
TAS does not compete with world models by simulating more reality. It governs whether simulated possibility is admissible as real action.
```

## 3. The Falsification Surface

The Falsification Surface acts as the "courtroom" of the TAS architecture. It is not designed to prove a state is "true" in a narrative sense, but rather to subject all proposed states to rigorous cryptographic falsification. Only states that cannot be falsified are admitted.

### The MNIST Torsion Bench
The MNIST Torsion Bench is a specialized testing environment within the Falsification Surface, designed to evaluate lineage-preserving accuracy under extreme torsional stress (e.g., adversarial inputs, high-velocity data streams).

* **Testable Invariant:** All proposed state updates must survive evaluation on the MNIST Torsion Bench without breaking their cryptographic lineage or violating Axiom P1 (Admissibility).
* **Inputs:** Proposed state update, historical lineage proofs, adversarial stress vectors.
* **Transformations:** Execution of the proposed state within the Torsion Bench environment while applying varying degrees of cryptographic and structural stress.
* **Failure Condition:** If the proposed state update fails to maintain its lineage-preserving accuracy or breaks under stress, it is falsified and irrevocably rejected by the courtroom.
